01 / Maps & layers

Bring your own data. Make it useful on the ground.

FieldOps is a local GIS workspace for your iPhone or iPad. Bring a drawing, an aerial image, a terrain model or a set of locations, then see your position in the context of that data. It is useful for a professional checking a project area and for an enthusiast exploring their own spatial datasets.

Import DXF, ESRI Shapefile, GeoJSON, GPX, KML, CSV and GeoTIFF/COG imagery and elevation data. Connect compatible XYZ, WMS, WMTS, WFS and ArcGIS REST services when online. Open supported attachments from Files, Mail or a cloud provider. Choose the correct source coordinate system so each dataset appears where it belongs.

Keep the map readable with layer visibility, opacity and ordering. Rename layers to match your work, fit the map to their extent, and remove data you no longer need. Search OpenAerialMap around a location and download available openly licensed aerial imagery for local use.

Example / Walk a project area

Load the site drawing and aerial image before leaving. In the field, compare planned features with your location, hide layers that obscure the view and save points for anything that needs a closer look.

02 / Coordinate systems

Work in the coordinates your project uses.

A useful field map starts with correctly positioned data. Set the position format, geodetic datum and default coordinate grid to suit your project. WGS84 latitude and longitude is available without a coordinate pack; supported regional grids use the applicable packs.

The Coordinate Transformer converts between source and destination EPSG systems. Batch conversion helps prepare several files in one operation, with individual results so a problem in one file does not hide successful conversions.

Choose export fields, target coordinate system, elevation units and precision for supported vector and waypoint exports. Download any required transformation grids before going offline, and check the source datum and grid zone against your project information.

Example / Use a supplied point list

A project gives you grid coordinates while your phone reports latitude and longitude. Configure the appropriate grid, import the points and use the same coordinate reference when you export your field records.

03 / Waypoints & tracks

Find the point. Keep a record of the journey.

Save your current position as a waypoint or enter a location manually. Import a CSV list with labels, X and Y coordinates and optional elevation, then organise them in movable, collapsible groups. Rename and reorder points, customise symbols and control visibility as the job develops.

Choose a destination and follow road manoeuvres towards the access point, then direct off-road bearing guidance for the final approach. Distance and ETA keep the journey clear, including when your inspection point, sample location or campsite lies beyond the mapped road.

Record tracks to retain a spatial record of where you walked or travelled. Lower-accuracy GPS sections are highlighted, helping you understand the quality of the recorded route. Unfinished tracks are checkpointed and can be recovered in a paused state for review, resumption or completion.

Example / Repeat an inspection

Import the inspection locations, navigate to each point and record the route between them. Keep the named waypoints so the next visit starts from the same reference locations.

In the vehicle / CarPlay

Your field maps, on the vehicle display.

Take FieldOps into the vehicle with a supported CarPlay system. Display visible project layers, including user-installed offline rasters and elevation models, so the journey has the same project context as your field map.

Browse saved waypoints and follow road-to-field guidance. Road manoeuvres guide you towards the access point, then direct off-road bearing guidance helps you approach a destination beyond the mapped road. The display brings together the next manoeuvre, remaining distance and ETA.

Request a saved destination with Siri and keep the navigation on the vehicle screen. Prepare the local terrain and raster files before heading out; remote map services and new downloads still need connectivity.

Example / Reach a remote work site

Save the site as a waypoint before departure. Follow the road portion on CarPlay, then use the off-road bearing and your installed project layers to understand the final approach.

On your wrist / Apple Watch

Make the quick field check without reaching for your phone.

The FieldOps Apple Watch companion keeps essential field tools close at hand. Check your position on a standard or satellite map, use the dedicated compass, save a waypoint, record a track and navigate to a saved destination.

Use bearing lock to hold a direction, find nearby waypoints or use Backtrack to help retrace your route. A live field dashboard brings key information together, while dictated notes let you capture an observation on the spot. The companion also includes safety check-ins and a remote shutter for the iPhone camera.

For geological fieldwork, the Watch geology compass records planes and lineations—strike and dip, or trend and plunge—and sends the measurement back to the FieldOps project on iPhone.

Example / Walk an outcrop

Record your track, mark a useful location and dictate a note from your wrist. Take a structural measurement with the geology compass and send it back to the iPhone project for review.

04 / Terrain · experimental tools

Understand the shape of the land before you walk it.

Import a georeferenced elevation GeoTIFF to explore the surface as hillshade, contours or a 3D terrain view. Orbit and zoom around the model, and adjust vertical exaggeration to make subtle changes easier to see. At 1×, the view uses physical scale.

Generate contours at a chosen interval, with every fifth line emphasised as an index contour. Gradient arrows show local downhill direction and slope. Draw an A–B cross-section to inspect how elevation changes along a line through the source terrain.

Compare Shortest, Balanced and Easiest terrain routes to explore slope-aware alternatives. These routes describe the terrain surface; they do not account for fences, access permission, road conditions or hazards.

Example / Plan a field traverse

Inspect a ridge in 3D, check a cross-section through the steepest ground and compare route options before deciding where to walk. Confirm the actual ground conditions during the visit.

More ways to bring terrain into the field

From imagery and survey data to a local surface.

These experimental workflows extend the terrain toolkit. Results depend on the georeferencing, elevation units and quality of the source data.

WebODM projects

Import a WebODM project ZIP and bring georeferenced orthophotos, DTM, DSM and DEM outputs into separate layers. Supported 3D meshes can use their original textures or a matched orthophoto drape. Each raster needs usable embedded coordinate information.

XYZ and LAS terrain generation

Build an elevation raster from supported XYZ text data or uncompressed LAS point clouds. Where sufficient classified ground points exist, the tool can use them; otherwise it uses the available samples. This is a way to create a working surface, not automatic bare-earth classification.

Raster and imagery context

Use an elevation model to understand shape and an overlapping image to understand what is on the ground. Supported imagery can be draped over the 3D surface; remote imagery needs a connection unless the required data is already available locally.

Check the source once

Confirm horizontal coordinates, vertical units, vertical datum and NoData values before interpreting a surface. A model with the wrong units or reference can look plausible while producing misleading heights, slopes and distances.

05 / Field capture

Keep the observation with its location.

Fieldwork produces more than points. Capture photographs with location and orientation information, add a note while the details are fresh and use photo waypoints to find the observation again on the map.

Configure the camera overlay with the date and time, coordinates, altitude, datum, angles, notes and a company logo. The instrument view brings azimuth, pitch, roll and a level cue into the same workflow, making it easier to document how the camera was oriented.

Use ground AR to view supported imported DXF lines in the camera view. Dense drawings may be simplified in AR while their full geometry remains available on the map. Available camera and sensor controls depend on your device.

Example / Record an asset condition

Save the asset location, capture a photograph with the relevant overlay and add a condition note. The record retains the context needed to understand where you were and what you were looking at.

Specialist field records

Capture structure, not just a position.

Geological observations

Record planes using right-hand-rule strike, dip and dip direction, or lineations using trend and plunge. Hold a reading, add notes and lithology, and retain location and altitude with the observation.

Stereonet & CSV

Review locally saved structural observations on a lower-hemisphere, equal-area stereonet. Export CSV records for further interpretation and reporting in your wider workflow.

LiDAR scene capture

On supported LiDAR devices, capture a short-range mesh of an outcrop, trench or asset. Start, pause, resume and finish a scan, then export PLY. Meshes use local AR coordinates and are not georeferenced or textured.

06 / Engineering toolkit · experimental

Ask practical questions of your terrain.

Use a selected elevation surface to explore locations, quantities, drainage and corridors. These tools are field decision aids; their results depend on the source model and inputs.

Sight-and-locate

Use the live reticle or enter bearing, elevation angle, instrument height and range to intersect a sight line with the terrain. Save the resulting target as a waypoint. The terrain model must cover the sight line; this tool locates an intersection rather than recognising an object.

Earthworks estimates

Compare the terrain with a constant formation level or an overlapping design surface. Review cut, fill, net volume and valid area to explore a preliminary scenario. These raster estimates are not certified quantities and do not include shrink, swell or compaction factors.

Drainage patterns

Generate a drainage vector layer from downhill flow directions across the elevation model. Adjust the contributing-area threshold to change the stream network shown. This describes terrain-based drainage potential, not rainfall, flow rate or flood extent.

Corridor assessment

Draw a centreline and set a corridor width to sample the centre and edges. Review length, ascent, descent, maximum grade and cross-slope when comparing potential alignments. Use the results for initial assessment before detailed route design.

Example / Assess a possible access corridor

Draw a candidate alignment, review its grade and cross-slope, inspect nearby drainage and compare an alternative. Take the result into the field to check constraints the elevation model cannot show.

07 / Offline work, export & backup

Prepare once. Keep working in the field.

FieldOps keeps your working data local. Download the files and coordinate resources you need before leaving coverage, then use saved data, device positioning and field tools on site.

Prepare for offline use

Import project files, check their positions and download required transformation grids in advance. Local files remain available without an office connection. Online basemaps, remote tile services and new downloads still require network access.

Export for the next step

Build supported waypoint and vector exports with the fields, target EPSG system, elevation units and precision you need. Use CSV and GeoJSON outputs in your wider GIS workflow, and export specialist observations or meshes in their supported formats.

Back up through Files

Choose a supported Files provider such as iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or Box. Enable automatic cloud backup and run the first Back Up Now. Backup runs while the app is active; upload timing is managed by iOS and the provider.

Help on the device

The in-app guide is available offline, so instructions remain accessible in the field. Positioning and sensor settings help you review how location, heading, units and overlays are configured before recording your work.

Before you head out

A few useful details.

Does FieldOps work without mobile coverage?

Local project files and supported field tools can be used offline. Prepare the required data and coordinate resources first. Remote map services and downloads need a connection, and cloud uploads wait for connectivity.

Do I need a LiDAR-equipped device?

LiDAR scene capture requires supported hardware. Imported terrain models are a separate workflow: you can inspect an existing elevation model without scanning it yourself. Available camera, AR and sensor controls depend on the device.

Are all coordinate systems included?

WGS84 latitude and longitude is always available. Regional coordinate grids use the applicable coordinate packs, and some transformations require an additional grid download. Check the app and App Store listing for current purchase options.

What does experimental mean for the terrain tools?

Terrain generation, WebODM and engineering workflows are evolving field decision aids. Check their outputs against known data and conditions on the ground. Feature availability can vary by app version; device GPS and sensor measurements are not a substitute for certified survey equipment.

Does batch conversion rewrite every supported file type?

No. Supported vector and survey data can be exported as target-system GeoJSON, and GeoTIFF conversion warps and resamples the raster. For MBTiles and LAS/LAZ, conversion produces a transformed coverage footprint rather than rewriting the tiles or point records. Terrain generation accepts supported uncompressed LAS, not compressed LAZ.
